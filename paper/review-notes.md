# Internal review of the TACO version

This is an internal review of the journal draft. It is not external peer review and not an acceptance prediction. It lists the concerns a TACO reviewer is likely to raise, what the manuscript and artifact now do about each, and what remains open.

| Concern | Response in the manuscript and artifact | Remaining limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A toy language: does the idea reach a real compiler? | ExactReduce, an LLVM 18 pass (Section 9), derives the facts from integer conversions in C and licenses reductions that the unmodified Clang vectorizer then speeds up (RQ7). It was run on four existing code bases and end to end on CPython's `audioop` (RQ9). | The pass handles add reductions only, not chains or identity rewrites; MatrixLang remains straight-line. |
| The headline speedups come from chain reordering, which any chain optimizer gets | The RQ4 tables add the algebraic contract (reordering without proofs). The text says the contribution is that a *strict* compiler may take these speedups, not the speedups themselves. | None. |
| Only synthetic workloads | RQ4 adds the Cora citation graph and MNIST, including an int8 classifier trained on MNIST. RQ7 uses the same data in C. RQ9 uses existing code. | Two public datasets; RQ9's code bases were chosen because they build without their build systems. |
| One machine, one compiler | RQ4 was repeated on a second machine with GCC 13 and Clang 18. Certified outputs are identical on every build, compiler and machine. | The second machine is a 4-core cloud instance, and its timings are noisier. |
| Speedups without uncertainty | Section 11 states the interquartile range of every build's runs relative to its median (median 6% on M1, 11% on M2; above 10 ms at most 20% and 37%) and the spread of RQ7's process medians, so that ratios near 1.05 read as noise. Generated by `analyze_spread.py`. | No confidence interval per speedup; the tables still show medians only. |
| Is the LLVM license sound beyond random tests? | Proposition 4.5 proves it. A stress suite is built from the proof obligation and includes trip counts past each guard's limit. Two mutants of the pass show that the checks are necessary (RQ8). | Testing covers the stress families, not every input; the proposition covers the rest. |
| Why not accumulate in integers? | ExactReduce now performs that rewrite itself (`EXACT_REDUCE_INTEGER=1`, Section 9): licensed sums of integers are accumulated in `i32` or `i64` and converted once, with a trip-count tier for `i32`. On M1 it runs the nine integer kernels 4.08–17.8x faster than strict Clang (median 10.2x), a median 102% (40–128%) of hand-written integer code, against 4.85x for the license (Figure 1). It changed none of 784 stress cases, matched strict on 20 cases at GEMM's 32-bit limit, and a mutant with a limit two trips larger changed 4. | The pass knows only the bounds the types and trip counts imply: on 2-walks, whose degrees the programmer knows to be below 169, hand-written code stays 2.5x faster. |
| Ordered (in-order) vector reductions keep the bits too | RQ7 times `-force-ordered-reductions`, which vectorizes 12 of the kernels' loops in source order: 1.02–2.25x (median 1.81x), strict bits on every integer kernel and stress case. | Measured on x86 (AVX2), which has no in-order vector add; targets with one (SVE `fadda`) may differ. |
| Too few TACO papers cited | The related work now places three verified TACO papers: Siso et al. 2019 (evaluating vectorizers by withdrawing information), Angerd et al. 2017 (controlled precision reduction), Chen and Singh 2013 (profile-guided float-to-fixed conversion, the unsound counterpart of the integer rewrite). | Three tangential references (PEP 594, the GCN paper as the Cora source, bitwidth analysis) were dropped to fit 20 pages. |
| How often does real code benefit? | RQ9 counts every add reduction LLVM recognizes in four code bases and classifies every decline. | Most scientific reductions sum floating-point data and are declined. The paper states this. |
| Soundness of certificates with weaker rewrites | Proposition 3.1 gives the induction behind the inheritance rule. RQ2 records the defect that motivated the rule. | The argument is on paper, not mechanised. |
| Is the theorem's exactness condition right at the edge of the range? | The definition of a fitting sub-chain and the free-reduction corollary now cap the bound at `DBL_MAX`; without the cap, scalars 2^1023, 2, 0 satisfy the inequality while (AB)C is NaN and A(BC) is 0. The implementation already rejected such bounds. | None known. |
| Is the compiler's bound arithmetic sound? | An exact-rational oracle found two understated bounds in the measured commit (`mul_up` lost an FMA residual below the subnormal range; the bounded coefficient was formed as fl(prod(1+gamma)) - 1). Both are repaired, the oracle is in `make test`, and Section 10 reports the evidence from `bound_repair.py`: all 174 example compilations byte-identical and all 128,015 deterministic records of RQ1–RQ3, RQ5 and RQ6 reproduced. | The oracle checks finite cases; the scaling argument is written, not mechanised. Timed studies (RQ4) were not rerun, since the generated C is unchanged. |
| Statistics and the RQ6 protocol | The protocol was recorded in the artifact before the confirmatory run; the paper now says so instead of "preregistered", since it is in no public registry. Holm-corrected, seed-cluster bootstrap; deviations stated. | The ablation cannot separate two mechanisms. |
| Authorship and AI use | Author details follow the authors' instructions. A generative-AI statement is drafted at the end of the manuscript. | Author approval, email, funding and interest declarations, and confirmation of the AI statement are pending. |

All numbers in the manuscript are generated by `tools/contracts/analyze.py`, `analyze_platforms.py`, `analyze_llvm.py`, `analyze_spread.py` and `bound_repair.py` from the raw files in `results/`.
